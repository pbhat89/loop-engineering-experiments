# House rule book

_Rewritten after UW-T22. Every entry is reviewer-settled practice from an application already decided in this run (UW-T01 through UW-T22). The starter manual governs anything this book is silent on. Where the book and the manual differ, the book is the reviewer's practice and the book wins. That matters in five places: private aviation takes a flat extra, not the manual's fallback debit (entry 6); family-history debits are dropped once the applicant is older than 60 (entry 12); a treated blood pressure carries the treatment charge only, whatever the reading, and that charge is neither topped up nor credited away (entry 11); where the cholesterol band and the ratio band disagree, the ratio governs and the cholesterol band is not charged (entry 15); and four or more charged cardiac tables carry an interaction charge on top of their own debits (entry 14)._

## How to use this book

Work every file in this order.

1. **Postponement checks** (entries 1 and 2): total cover against the income multiple; any DUI conviction and its year. If either trips, the decision is `postpone`, rating class null, no modifiers. Still finish steps 2 to 4, because the drivers on a postponed file (entry 3) are every factor that would have charged, taken a credit against the total, or carried a modifier.

2. **Referred items.** List every line the manual refers to underwriting judgement and resolve each under entries 5 to 15. First make sure the line really is referred: the manual's referral words are narrow, and a line outside them is simply charged on its table (apnea with no CPAP in use, enzymes at or above twice normal, two or more moving violations, a class B or C occupation on its own, an A1c with no diagnosis, a family event on an applicant of 60 or under, tobacco quit within five years). Two referrals are easy to under-read because they are printed as general remarks: two lipid tables pointing at different bands (entry 15) and a combination of cardiac risk factors (entries 13 and 14). One is easy to over-work: a treated blood pressure is referred, and the settled answer is the manual's own treatment charge and nothing more (entry 11). Where an entry applies, follow it instead of the manual's table, instead of the manual's fallback, and instead of your own reasoning. Noticing a referral, writing that judgement was used, and then charging the raw table anyway is the same error as not noticing it (UW-T10, UW-T18); so is noticing it and treating it as a name when it carries a charge (UW-T20). Where the file sits near a settled point but not on it, lean the way the settled cases lean (UW-T17, UW-T20). Where nothing is referred, say so in one line (entry 19). Each referred line resolves on its own: a credit on one line does not soften another, a charge on one line does not withdraw a credit on another, and a modifier on one line leaves the debits on every other line exactly as they were (UW-T21).

3. **Tables, interaction and class.** Charge each table exactly as written, on the reading as entries 7 to 12 and 15 leave it. The driving record is two tables (entry 4). Blood pressure is one charge, never two (entry 11). Lipids are two charges when the bands agree and one charge, the ratio's, when they disagree (entry 15). Aviation is a flat extra, not debits (entry 6). Count the charged cardiac tables (entries 13 and 14): at four or more, add the interaction charge. Take any credit (entry 10). Read the class off the manual's section 2 bands, lower edges included: 25 is Standard, 50 and 75 are Table 2, 100 is Table 4, 150 is Table 6, 200 is Table 8. A modifier does not move the class: the class is read off the debits alone and the modifier rides beside it, so a file with one first-band charge and an aviation flat extra is Standard, accept with modification (UW-T21). On a zero-debit file, work the preferred classes under entry 18; any debit at all takes the file to entry 21.

4. **Drivers** under entry 16. The reviewer checks the material factors as closely as the class; a correct class with wrong drivers is still a mismatch, and a correct drivers list does not save a wrong class (UW-T06, UW-T20). A short list is right when the file is light: two drivers on UW-T21 (tobacco, aviation) and one on UW-T22 (blood pressure treatment) each matched the reviewer's list exactly.

## What a referral line means, on twenty-two files

- **Not permission to charge the full table debit** (UW-T01, UW-T10, UW-T18: referred items charged at face value came out too severe). Where the file documents a mitigating fact the reviewer credits or adjusts; where a hazard can be carved out of cover the reviewer uses a rider; where a hazard is to be priced, it is priced as a flat extra (UW-T10, UW-T21).
- **Not permission to wave the item through** (UW-T02, UW-T06: cover above the multiple and a recent DUI were each accepted with a reassuring note; the reviewer postponed both). Where the referral turns on evidence not on the file, or on time, postpone.
- **Not an instruction to invent a charge no table carries** (UW-T03, UW-T13, UW-T14, UW-T18, UW-T22: a mild cardiac cluster with one charged table; nothing added, all confirmed. UW-T21: readings that missed preferred thresholds inside their zero bands; nothing referred on the cardiac side, nothing added).
- **Not a name only, either.** The one referral that carries its own charge is the cardiac combination at four or more charged tables: UW-T20 named it and did not charge it, and came out one step too lenient (entry 14).
- **Not a reason to credit a charge the manual has already sized for the state** (UW-T17, UW-T22: a treated blood pressure is referred, and both times the answer was the manual's treatment charge standing alone, neither credited to zero for control nor topped up for the reading).
- **Not a reason to soften a heavy file** (UW-T04: five charged cardiac tables plus class C, Table 8 confirmed).
- **Not something to hunt for on a clean or nearly clean file** (UW-T05, UW-T07, UW-T11, UW-T15, UW-T16, UW-T19: nothing referred, said so in one sentence, each confirmed).
- **Not a reason to touch lines that are not referred** (UW-T08, UW-T12, UW-T13, UW-T14, UW-T17, UW-T18, UW-T20, UW-T21, UW-T22: credited, dropped, adjusted, modified or judgement-confirmed lines sat beside other lines and neither moved the other).
- **Not a reason to stop reading the file once it is postponed** (UW-T09: eight drivers on a postponed file, all matched).
- **Not discharged by finding the file just outside the book's own boundary and withholding the credit** (UW-T17: diabetes diagnosed at about 49 called not late-onset; the reviewer credited it. UW-T20: an applicant of 63 with two family events, credited against a book boundary drawn at 65; the reviewer confirmed the credit and draws the line at older than 60). A settled point is where a credit has been seen, not the edge of where it is given.
- **An adjustment is made whichever way it moves the file** (UW-T14, UW-T17: the weight add-back raised the class both times; UW-T20: the interaction charge raised it).
- **A charged line is charged once and called by what was charged** (UW-T17, UW-T22: blood pressure treatment; UW-T18, UW-T20: lipid ratio).
- **A material factor is a factor that carried a charge, took a credit against the total, carried a modifier, or forced the decision, and nothing else** (entry 16).

A referred item therefore resolves in one of six ways. **Credit** it when the file documents the mitigating fact (entries 7, 8, 10, 12). **Adjust how the line is read** before the band is charged (entries 9, 15). **Convert it to a modifier** (entries 5, 6; UW-T01, UW-T10, UW-T21). **Postpone** when the question cannot be answered from the file (entries 1, 2). **Rate on the tables as they stand** where every reading is already priced and at most three cardiac tables charge (entry 13), or where the manual already carries the one charge that fits the state (entry 11; UW-T17, UW-T22). **Charge the interaction on top** where four or more cardiac tables charge (entry 14). The only rated file corrected as too lenient (UW-T20) omitted that last step; every other correction on a rated file has been too severe or has been to the drivers.

## 1. Total cover above the income multiple: postpone

**When it applies:** Face amount applied for plus cover already in force exceeds the section 14 multiple for the applicant's age (18-30 30x | 31-40 25x | 41-50 20x | 51-60 15x | 61+ 10x). Run this on every file before rating anything else; in-force cover counts in full.

**What to do:** Decision `postpone`, rating class null, no modifiers. Do not accept at any class with the excess explained away. Say the file is postponed for financial evidence (justification of the amount, income verification, purpose of cover), and state the total, the multiple and the excess. Name face amount to income among the drivers, with every factor that would have rated (entry 3).

**Why:** UW-T02 (age 40, $2.0M total against a 25x ceiling of $1.7M, otherwise a clean Standard) was accepted with a paragraph on why the excess was tolerable; the reviewer postponed. Whether the amount is justified is not answered by anything on the file. No tolerance above the multiple is known; do not invent one.

## 2. Recent DUI conviction: postpone

**When it applies:** A DUI conviction on the file that is recent. A conviction two years before the application (2024 against a 2026 file) was recent on both files seen (UW-T06, UW-T09). It trips on its own: a clean MVR, normal liver chemistry and no alcohol history do not switch it off, and a heavy file does not turn it back into a rating.

**What to do:** Decision `postpone`, rating class null, no modifiers. Do not charge the manual's 50 and read a class. Set the conviction year against the application date; say the file is postponed for the conviction to age and for the record around it (disposition, licence status, any alcohol assessment or treatment); say that normal liver chemistry and no declared alcohol history do not settle the question. Then finish the file: place cover inside the multiple (entry 17) and say what would have charged, been credited or been modified. Name 'DUI' among the drivers with every factor that would have rated (entry 3).

**Boundary:** The manual's 50 is the rate for a conviction that is not recent. Treat a DUI within the last two years as recent; for an older one, charge 50, name 'DUI', and say judgement was used on recency. Moving violations are a separate table (entry 4), never a reason to postpone, and no DUI means this entry has nothing to act on however many violations sit beside it (UW-T15, UW-T17, UW-T19 at three each; all rated).

**Why:** UW-T06 (DUI convicted 2024, untreated apnea, all else zero) was accepted at Table 4 and postponed on review. UW-T09 (DUI 2024, two violations, four charged cardiac readings, private pilot) was postponed with eight drivers and confirmed. Recency is settled by time, and nothing on today's file stands in for it.

## 3. Drivers on a postponed file

**When it applies:** Any file postponed under entry 1 or 2.

**What to do:** List the postponement reason and every factor that would have been material had the file been rated, built exactly as entry 16 builds them: one per table that would have charged, in the reviewer's names; 'aviation' or the rider avocation where a modifier would have attached; 'late-onset diabetes credit' where entry 10 would apply; 'cardiac risk interaction' where entry 14 would charge it; the lipid line as entry 15 would leave it. Rating class null, modifiers empty. Do not list zero-band readings, credited lines or reassuring facts.

**Why:** UW-T02: face amount to income and tobacco. UW-T06: DUI and sleep apnea. UW-T09: DUI, blood pressure reading, total cholesterol, lipid ratio, A1c, moving violations, aviation and cardiac risk interaction; the reviewer's list matched exactly. A postpone defers the class, not the underwriting.

## 4. Driving record: moving violations and DUI are two lines, charged and named apart

**When it applies:** Every file; section 11.

**What to do:** Zero or one violation in three years is the zero band: charge nothing, name nothing. Two violations is the first charged band and three or more the second: charge as written and name 'moving violations'. A DUI is its own line: recent, postpone (entry 2); not recent, charge 50 and name 'DUI'. Where both charge, name both.

**Boundary:** Violations are never a referred item and never a reason to postpone, whatever the count: three in three years is the second charged band, not the manual's 'recent motoring conviction', which is the DUI line (UW-T15, UW-T17, UW-T19). Two or more violations carry a debit, so the file never reaches the preferred criteria. A single violation on an otherwise zero-debit file leaves it at Standard by criterion (entry 18); beside a charge elsewhere it is nothing (UW-T12). A clean record (UW-T20, UW-T21, UW-T22) is nothing and is not a credit against anything. The violations line is not a cardiac table.

**Why:** One violation: zero and unnamed on UW-T08 and UW-T12. Two: charged and named on UW-T09 (postponed) and UW-T10. Three: the only charge on UW-T15 (Table 2 on exactly 50, confirmed), among six factors on UW-T17, and beside class B on UW-T19 (Table 2 on 75, confirmed).

## 5. Occupation class: charge as written and name 'occupation class'; a hazardous avocation beside a class C occupation becomes a rider, not a flat extra

**When it applies:** Every file: section 9 for the occupation alone, and the section 10 referral where a class C occupation sits with an avocation section 10 would charge (amateur motorsport, private aviation, scuba beyond 60 ft, technical rock or ice climbing).

**What to do, occupation alone:** Class A is zero: charge nothing, name nothing (UW-T21 and UW-T22, regional sales manager both). Class B: charge as written and name 'occupation class' (UW-T19 and UW-T20, site foreman). Class C: charge as written and name 'occupation class' (UW-T04 logging feller, UW-T13 structural steel erector). No class is a referred item on its own: nothing about judgement, no softening because the rest of the file is clean, no adding because the work sounds dangerous. Class B always carries a debit, so the Standard Plus criterion that admits class B is never met in practice. Occupation class is not a cardiac table.

**What to do, class C with a hazardous avocation:** Charge the occupation class debit. For the avocation, attach an exclusion rider naming the hazard; no flat extra and no avocation debits. The rider makes the decision `accept_with_modification`. Name 'occupation class' and the avocation among the drivers: the avocation is material though it carries no charge.

**Boundary:** A hazardous avocation with a class A or B occupation is not this combination: private aviation goes to entry 6; the other section 10 avocations charge as their table reads. A pastime section 10 does not list (stained glass, chillies, crime novels, a book club, a choir, a pub quiz, classical guitar, beekeeping, sourdough, a pet, a rugby season ticket) is not an avocation and is never named; the file's 'also on file' line is where these sit, and it is incidental every time. On UW-T21 the guitar was nothing and the flying beside it was entry 6; on UW-T22 the season ticket was nothing. Do not reason a hobby into a hazard or steadiness of character into a rating.

**Why:** Class B confirmed on UW-T19 and UW-T20; class C on UW-T04 and UW-T13. On UW-T01 (high-voltage lineman plus club saloon racing) the $5.00 flat extra was struck and replaced by a motorsport exclusion rider: once one hazardous exposure is priced through the occupation class, the second is removed from cover rather than charged on top.

## 6. Private aviation without a hazardous occupation: first-band flat extra, no debits, never a decline

**When it applies:** The applicant flies as a private pilot and the occupation is class A or B. This replaces the manual's fallback of 50.

**What to do:** No debits for aviation on any table. Attach a flat extra at the first band (amount_per_1000 2.50) with the detail naming private aviation. Decision `accept_with_modification`; the class is read off the other tables and the flat extra does not move it up or down. Name 'aviation'. Say aviation was considered under judgement, the hours are modest, and it carries a first-band flat extra and no rating debits. Never decline for the flying alone.

**Boundary:** Modest hours are settled at 77 a year on about 1,100 total with no aerobatics (UW-T21, class A, confirmed at Standard) and 82 a year (UW-T10, confirmed at Table 6); 75 a year was named on the postponed UW-T09. A logbook in the hundreds or low thousands of total hours changes nothing. A heavier programme, aerobatics or a very thin logbook is untested: still no debits, still a flat extra, say judgement was used on the band and do not step up without a case. With a class C occupation, entry 5 converts the avocation to a rider. Aviation is not a cardiac table. A pilot's file never reaches a preferred class, because every preferred class requires no hazardous avocation: a pilot with every table at zero is Standard with the flat extra (untested; on UW-T21 tobacco had already closed the preferred classes and the total of 25 read Standard on its own). 'Avocation: none' means nothing to do here (UW-T20, UW-T22).

**Why:** UW-T10 charged the fallback 50 and read Table 8; the reviewer: one step too severe, accept with modification at Table 6 with a first-band flat extra; private aviation at modest hours takes a flat extra rather than debits. UW-T21 (male 57, whole life $250,000, 77 hrs/yr, otherwise a tobacco-only file) applied the rule at Standard and the reviewer confirmed it with tobacco and aviation the material factors.

## 7. Sleep apnea: credited to zero with documented CPAP compliance, charged as written without it

**When it applies:** Sleep apnea is diagnosed.

**What to do:** With documented CPAP compliance: no debit, not a driver; one sentence that treated apnea was credited to zero under judgement. With no CPAP in use or compliance not documented: charge the manual's 50, name 'sleep apnea', say it is charged as written.

**Boundary:** 'No CPAP in use' is untreated apnea however clean the rest of the file; it is not the 'treated sleep-disordered breathing' referral. Do not postpone for a sleep study, and add nothing for apnea beside an obese build. Any diagnosis closes all three preferred classes, so a file whose only abnormality is compliant apnea is a zero-debit Standard (untested). No apnea reported means nothing, and a 'CPAP compliant: false' field beside 'no apnea' means nothing (UW-T20, UW-T21, UW-T22). Apnea is not a cardiac table.

**Why:** Compliant apnea credited on UW-T01 and UW-T12 (Standard, liver enzymes the only factor). Untreated apnea charged as written and confirmed on UW-T06, UW-T08, UW-T14, UW-T17 and UW-T18.

## 8. Liver chemistry: a mild rise with no alcohol history is credited to zero; at or above twice normal it is charged and named 'liver enzymes'

**When it applies:** Section 12: elevated enzymes, a history of alcohol treatment, or both.

**What to do:** Elevated below twice the upper limit of normal with no alcohol history or treatment is the borderline chemistry the manual refers: credit to zero, not a driver, one sentence that it was treated as incidental. Elevated at or above twice normal with no alcohol history: not borderline, no credit; charge as written and name 'liver enzymes'; add nothing for the size of the rise, do not postpone for a repeat panel. The empty alcohol history is a fact the credit needs, not a credit of its own. Normal chemistry is zero and unnamed (UW-T20 through UW-T22 among many) and does not offset a DUI. Any history of alcohol treatment (untested): charge the treatment debit as written and name it; a mild rise beside it is not credited, because the credit exists for a rise with no alcohol behind it.

**Boundary:** The credit and the charge are local to the liver line (UW-T08, UW-T12). A mild rise credited to zero still closes all three preferred classes by criterion. Liver enzymes are not a cardiac table.

**Why:** Mild elevation treated as incidental on UW-T01 and UW-T08. Above twice normal charged and confirmed on UW-T11 (Table 2 with tobacco) and UW-T12 (Standard, alone).

## 9. Weight lost in the last twelve months: add half back before reading the build band, whichever way it moves the file

**When it applies:** The twelve-month weight change is a loss, whatever its size and whatever the printed BMI. This is the settled answer to the section 3 referral for a recent change in build.

**What to do:** Add half the year's loss back onto the current weight, recompute the BMI at the file's height, and read the section 3 band off the recomputed BMI. Where the band charges, name 'build'; 'weight change' or 'weight loss' is never named. One sentence: weight down so many pounds, half added back under judgement, adjusted weight and BMI so much, band read accordingly. Do it whichever way it moves the file; do not credit the loss, do not skip it because it raises the class or because the printed BMI is normal, do not postpone for the weight to prove stable.

**Boundary:** Half is the fraction for a 16 lb loss (UW-T10), a 28 lb loss (UW-T14) and a 17 lb loss on a printed BMI of 26.9 (UW-T17, where the add-back put a charge on a file whose printed build carried none and the reviewer listed 'build' first). Do not scale the fraction. A stable weight is not a recent change in build and is read as it stands (UW-T20, UW-T21, UW-T22 and most files). Weight gained in the year is untested: read the current BMI, subtract nothing, say judgement was used. Build on the adjusted weight is one cardiac table for entries 13 and 14, and is the figure the preferred criteria are read against.

**Why:** UW-T10's markup: weight lost in the past year is added back at half before the build band is read. UW-T14 and UW-T17 repeated it word for word, the class rising both times and confirmed. Recent loss is not yet durable.

## 10. Type 2 diabetes first diagnosed in later middle age with a well-controlled A1c: charge the A1c, take a credit, name both

**When it applies:** A type 2 diabetes diagnosis; the diagnosis year puts the applicant in later middle age at the time; the A1c is well controlled. Settled: diagnosed at about 54 with A1c 6.6 (UW-T10) and at about 49 with A1c 6.8 (UW-T17), both diet controlled. Present age and years since diagnosis do not matter.

**What to do:** Charge the A1c on its section 6 table. Take a credit against the total for late-onset, well-controlled disease and read the class off the credited total. Name 'A1c' and 'late-onset diabetes credit' as two drivers. One sentence: diagnosed in such a year at such an age, controlled by such means to such an A1c; A1c charged; late-onset credit taken under judgement.

**Size of the credit:** The markup gives no point value. On both settled files the uncredited total was exactly 200 and the reviewer's class was Table 6, one band below. Work the credit as one band below what the uncredited total reads, and say the size is judgement.

**Boundary:** An A1c with no diagnosis is never this entry, whatever the reading: in the zero band it is nothing, not named even where it misses a preferred threshold (UW-T21 at 6.0, UW-T22 at 5.7); in a charged band with no diagnosis (UW-T04 at 6.6; UW-T09 and UW-T20 at 6.8) it is charged with no credit and named 'A1c'. Age at diagnosis: 49 and 54 are settled, so from about 45 take the credit and say judgement was used on the age; the twenties or thirties are not later middle age, charge with no credit. Control: the whole 6.5-6.9 band under diet is well controlled; oral medication in that band is untested, the condition is control not means, take the credit and say so; 7.0 or above with a diagnosis is untested, charge and take no credit. A diagnosed diabetic with an A1c at or under 6.4 has nothing to charge or credit; the diagnosis closes all three preferred classes. The credited A1c line stays a charged cardiac table for entries 13 and 14.

**Why:** UW-T10 was corrected to take the credit; UW-T17 withheld it by drawing the age line at the early fifties and lost a band, the reviewer correcting it in the same words with 'late-onset diabetes credit' among the factors. Do not draw the line tight again.

## 11. Blood pressure on treatment: the treatment charge only, named 'blood pressure treatment'; the reading is neither charged on top nor credited away

**When it applies:** Blood pressure on treatment, whatever year treatment began and whatever the reading. Settled twice: 140/85 on treatment since 2016, called 'treated and controlled' (UW-T17), and 141/83 on treatment since 2019 (UW-T22). Both readings would sit in the first charged band of the untreated systolic table; on treatment neither was charged.

**What to do:** Charge the on-treatment 25 and nothing else for blood pressure. Do not read the untreated table against a treated reading. Do not credit the treatment charge to zero because the reading is controlled: the manual's referral for a treated condition now at target resolves as the treatment charge standing alone. Name 'blood pressure treatment', never 'blood pressure reading'. Do not write that a reading like 140/85 or 141/83 on treatment is not at target. One sentence, in the wording confirmed on UW-T22: treated since such a year; referred; judgement used; the treatment charge stands alone and the reading is not charged on top. Where the treatment charge is the only debit on the file, the total of 25 reads Standard, the decision is `accept` with no modifier, and 'blood pressure treatment' is the one driver (UW-T22, confirmed whole).

**Boundary:** Untreated is the other case: charge the worse of the systolic and diastolic bands and name 'blood pressure reading' (UW-T04; UW-T09 at 147/83; UW-T20 at 148/84, first band on the systolic, confirmed). An untreated reading inside the zero band is nothing and unnamed (UW-T21 at 135/84); it only closes preferred classes. A treated reading from about 149 systolic or 95 diastolic is untested: charge the treatment 25, say judgement was used on whether the reading charges on top, and do not add it without a case. How long the applicant has been treated changes nothing (since 2016 and since 2019 are both settled). Any treatment closes all three preferred classes; one clause saying so is enough, and the criteria are not worked on a charged file. Blood pressure, treated or untreated-and-charged, is one cardiac table for entries 13 and 14.

**Why:** UW-T17 charged 25 but named 'blood pressure reading' and wrote that the reading was not at target; the reviewer's factor was 'blood pressure treatment' and the markup: treated and controlled blood pressure carries the treatment charge only. UW-T22 (male 40, 30-year term $500,000, BMI 23.2 stable, never tobacco, 141/83 treated since 2019, cholesterol 214 with ratio 4.1, A1c 5.7 with no diagnosis, no family events, class A, no avocation, clean record, normal liver chemistry, no apnea) applied the entry as the sole charge, read Standard, named the one driver, and the reviewer confirmed it: correct, the material factor was blood pressure treatment. The debit is the manual's own; the referral settles that nothing is added to it and nothing taken from it.

## 12. Family-history events on an applicant older than 60: dropped, not named

**When it applies:** A parent or sibling had an MI, stroke or cancer before 60, so section 8 would charge it, and the applicant is older than 60 (61 or over). The manual's line that the applicant's own age never changes the charge describes the table; the referral for applicants past the qualifying age wins, and the reviewer's rule is that family-history debits are dropped once the applicant is older than the qualifying age.

**What to do:** No debit for family history, however many events, not a driver. One sentence: the applicant is older than the family-history qualifying age, judgement was used, the family-history debits are dropped. Charge every other table as written; the credit is local.

**Boundary:** Settled at 63 with two events (UW-T20: father stroke at 49, mother pancreatic cancer at 58, both dropped) and at 65 with one (UW-T13). The book once held the credit back below 65; that boundary is gone. Applicant 60 or under: charge 25 per event capped at 50 and name 'family history' (UW-T04); at exactly 60 the applicant is not older than 60, so charge and say judgement was used. Do not postpone for records, and add nothing back for the kind of event. A dropped line is not a charged cardiac table for entries 13 and 14. On a zero-debit file a dropped event still counts against the preferred criteria: one closes Preferred and Preferred Plus, two close Standard Plus as well. No event on the file means nothing to act on at any age (UW-T21 at 57, UW-T22 at 40, no events).

**Why:** UW-T13 (65, father's stroke at 55) was credited and confirmed with build and occupation class the factors. UW-T20 (63, two events) was credited and the reviewer confirmed the credit in general terms. An applicant who has lived past the age that defines an early event has outrun the risk the charge prices.

## 13. Cardiac risk factors with three or fewer charged cardiac tables: charge the tables, add nothing, do not name the interaction

**When it applies:** Two or more cardiac risk readings sit together but, once entries 9, 10, 11, 12 and 15 have settled how each line is read, no more than three cardiac tables charge. The cardiac tables are: build (on the adjusted weight), blood pressure (reading or treatment), total cholesterol (only where its band agrees with the ratio's and it is charged), lipid ratio, A1c, and family history (only on an applicant of 60 or under). A dropped family event, an overridden cholesterol band and a zero-band reading are not charged tables. Tobacco, apnea, liver enzymes, the driving record, occupation class and aviation are never cardiac tables. Section 16 refers combinations of cardiac risk factors, so any such cluster is a referred item.

**What to do:** Charge what each table charges and nothing more: no cluster debit, no flat extra, no postponement for tests, no credit for the combination. Take any entry 10 credit. Read the class off the total. Name only the factors that carried a debit; never 'cardiac risk interaction' for one, two or three tables. Say the combination was considered under judgement and nothing was added, or simply that one cardiac table charges and nothing is added. Count the cardiac tables after entries 12 and 15 have resolved the family and lipid lines, not before.

**Boundary:** Settled with one charged table (UW-T03, UW-T13, UW-T14 on build; UW-T18 on the ratio; UW-T22 on blood pressure treatment, with cholesterol 214, ratio 4.1 and A1c 5.7 in their zero bands beside it), two (UW-T10: build and A1c; the rationale named the interaction and the reviewer's six factors did not) and three (UW-T17: build, blood pressure treatment, credited A1c; again named and again not among the reviewer's six). Where no cardiac table charges there is no cluster at all: zero-band build, pressure, lipids and A1c beside one another are not a combination even with a low HDL or readings that miss a preferred threshold by a point (UW-T15, UW-T16, UW-T19; UW-T21 at BMI 25.2, 135/84 untreated, cholesterol 197 with ratio 3.5 and A1c 6.0). Nothing referred, nothing added, nothing named; one line that no cardiac table charges is enough. Non-cardiac charges beside the cluster do not change the count. At four or more, entry 14.

**Why:** On every file with one to three charged cardiac tables the reviewer confirmed the charged factors and only those, and the class read off the plain total (credited under entry 10 where it applied). The combination referral does not manufacture a charge below four tables, and it does not exist at all below one.

## 14. Four or more charged cardiac tables: an interaction charge on top of the individual debits, named 'cardiac risk interaction'

**When it applies:** Four or more of the cardiac tables listed in entry 13 charge on the same file, or would charge on a file being postponed. Settled at four (UW-T20: build, blood pressure reading, lipid ratio, A1c) and five (UW-T04: blood pressure reading, total cholesterol, lipid ratio, A1c, family history on an applicant under 60); named at four on the postponed UW-T09.

**What to do:** Charge each cardiac table on its own table as written, add the non-cardiac tables, then add an interaction charge on top for the combination. Read the class off the total with the interaction in it. Name every charged factor, one per table, and add 'cardiac risk interaction' as a driver of its own. Say in one sentence that four (or five) cardiac tables charge and an interaction charge was added under judgement. Do not treat the interaction as a name only: that was the UW-T20 error and it cost a band. Do not postpone for repeat readings when the readings are on the file, and do not decline on judgement below the decline band. An A1c in a charged band with no diagnosis is charged and the absence of a diagnosis is not a credit; an untreated reading in a charged band is charged as a reading; a family event on an applicant of 60 or under is charged.

**Size of the charge:** The markup gives no point value. What is settled: on UW-T20 the individual debits summed to 175, the top of Table 6, and the reviewer's class was Table 8, so the interaction lifted the total at least one band; on UW-T04 the individual debits already summed into Table 8 and the reviewer's class was Table 8, so the interaction did not carry that total into the decline band. The one charge on the manual's ladder that fits both is a single first-band step: add one first-band debit for the interaction, read the class, and say the size is judgement. It did not grow at five tables on UW-T04; do not scale it.

**Boundary:** Count the tables after entries 12 and 15 have resolved the family and lipid lines: on UW-T20 the cholesterol band was overridden and the family events dropped, and four tables still charged. A charged A1c carrying the entry 10 credit still counts as a table. Three tables is entry 13 whatever their bands. A total that the interaction alone would carry into the decline band is untested: rate as the ladder reads and say judgement was used on the size. On a postponed file do the same work without a class (entry 3).

**Why:** UW-T20 (female 63, 30-year term, $900,000 total cover inside the 10x multiple; BMI 30.6 stable, 148/84 untreated, cholesterol 329 with ratio 5.7, A1c 6.8 with no diagnosis, tobacco quit within five years with a negative screen, site foreman, two family events dropped for age, no avocation, clean record, normal liver chemistry, no apnea) was charged build 25, blood pressure reading 25, lipid ratio 25, A1c 50, tobacco 25 and occupation class 25; the total of 175 was read as Table 6 and the interaction was named but not charged. The reviewer: one step too lenient; accept at Table 8; material factors build, blood pressure reading, lipid ratio, A1c, tobacco, occupation class and cardiac risk interaction; several cardiac risk factors appearing together carry an interaction charge on top of the individual debits. UW-T04 was confirmed at Table 8 with the same seven-factor shape. The interaction is a charge and a driver at four or more, and neither at three or fewer.

## 15. Lipids: where the total cholesterol band and the ratio band disagree, the ratio governs and the cholesterol band is not charged; where they agree, both charge and both are named

**When it applies:** Every file; section 5. Put the total cholesterol on its band (up to 240 zero; 241-280 first; 281-320 second; 321+ third) and the ratio on its band (up to 5.0 zero; 5.1-6.5 first; 6.6+ second) and compare.

**What to do:**

- **Both in the zero band:** charge nothing, name nothing, even if the HDL is low or the cholesterol closed a preferred class (UW-T15, UW-T16, UW-T17, UW-T19, UW-T21, UW-T22 at 214 with ratio 4.1).
- **Both in the same charged band:** the tables agree and nothing is referred. Charge both and name 'total cholesterol' and 'lipid ratio' as two drivers (UW-T04, UW-T09, both first band). Both in the second band is untested; charge both, name both, say judgement was used.
- **In different bands:** the section 5 referral, settled: the ratio governs. Charge the ratio's band and nothing for total cholesterol; name 'lipid ratio' only, never 'total cholesterol'. One sentence: cholesterol such, ratio such, bands disagree, judgement used, the ratio governs and the cholesterol band is not charged. Do not charge both and write that judgement was used (UW-T18); do not charge the worse; do not split the difference.

**Boundary:** Settled disagreements run cholesterol above ratio: 323 against 5.3 (UW-T18) and 329 against 5.7 (UW-T20) were each charged the first-band 25, not the third-band 75. A cholesterol of 321 or more can never agree with the ratio and is always charged on the ratio. Cholesterol in a charged band with the ratio at zero, or the ratio above the cholesterol, has no case: the ratio governs, charge its band (which may be nothing), say judgement was used. The lipid line is one cardiac table for entries 13 and 14 when the ratio governs and two when both charge (UW-T04 counted five with both in; UW-T20 counted four with the ratio alone). A low HDL is never a factor of its own. The line resolves on its own and softens nothing else.

**Why:** UW-T18 charged 75 for cholesterol and 25 for the ratio and came out one step too severe; the reviewer: the ratio governs, the cholesterol band is not charged. UW-T20 applied the rule and the reviewer restated it while confirming 'lipid ratio' among the factors. The ratio already carries the HDL; a high total against a good HDL is priced by the ratio alone.

## 16. Naming drivers

**When it applies:** Every file.

**What to do:** The drivers are the material factors the reviewer will list; build them the same way.

- One driver per table that carried a debit (or would have, on a postponed file), by factor not number, in the reviewer's words: 'build', 'blood pressure reading' (untreated), 'blood pressure treatment' (treated), 'total cholesterol' (only where charged, entry 15), 'lipid ratio', 'A1c', 'family history' (only where charged, entry 12), 'occupation class' (class B or C), 'tobacco', 'sleep apnea', 'liver enzymes', 'moving violations', 'DUI'.
- One driver per modifier: 'aviation' (entry 6); the avocation for a rider (entry 5).
- 'late-onset diabetes credit' beside 'A1c' where entry 10 credits. A credit that zeroes a line (entries 7, 8, 12) is not named, and neither is a cholesterol band the ratio overrode.
- 'cardiac risk interaction' where entry 14 charges it (four or more cardiac tables), never for fewer.
- On a postponed file, the postponement reason plus every factor that would have rated (entry 3).
- Never a reading that carried no debit, a reading that only closed a higher preferred class, a credited or dropped line, an overridden band, or an incidental detail: a hobby, a pet, a season ticket, anything in the file's 'also on file' line, a stable weight, a normal build, a clean record or a single violation, tobacco never used or quit over five years, a negative nicotine screen, normal liver chemistry, an empty alcohol history, compliant apnea, no apnea, no family history, a class A occupation, no avocation, a low HDL with the ratio at zero, cover inside the multiple, years on treatment, the applicant's age or sex or the product.
- Zero tables charged, not postponed, no modifier: an empty list; the preferred criteria belong in the rationale.

**Why:** The reviewer lists the material factors on every file and faults the drivers in every direction: too few (UW-T04), too many (UW-T05, UW-T10, UW-T18), wrong names (UW-T17). Seven on UW-T20 matched the reviewer's seven exactly while the class did not, and UW-T06's drivers matched on a file that should have been postponed: a right list is necessary, not sufficient. Two on UW-T21 (tobacco, aviation) and one on UW-T22 (blood pressure treatment) matched exactly on files the reviewer confirmed whole: a light file has a short list, and nothing is added to make it look worked.

## 17. Cover inside the income multiple: one line, not a driver

**When it applies:** Face amount plus cover in force is at or under the multiple for the applicant's age.

**What to do:** State in one line that total cover sits inside the multiple (total, multiple, ceiling), run the DUI check (entry 2), and go on to the tables. Not a judgement item, not a driver, never a reason to postpone. In-force cover as large as or larger than the face applied for changes nothing inside the multiple. Age 31 to 40 is 25x; 41 to 50 is 20x; 51 to 60 is 15x, age 60 included; 61 and over is 10x.

**Why:** Every file other than UW-T02 has been inside the multiple and no financial question arose (for instance UW-T22: $500,000 against a 25x ceiling of $2,125,000 at 40; UW-T21: $250,000 against a 15x ceiling of $2.1M at 57; UW-T20: $900,000 against a 10x ceiling of $1.75M at 63; UW-T13: $600,000 against $1.75M at 65; UW-T19: $1,000,000 against $1.7M at 49). UW-T06 and UW-T09 were postponed on the DUI instead, which is why the two postponement checks run independently.

## 18. Zero-debit file: read the preferred class off its criteria, and the drivers are none

**When it applies:** Every table reads zero, outright or because a referred item was credited, dropped or overridden to zero; neither postponement check trips; no modifier.

**What to do:** Decision `accept`. Work the three preferred classes from the top, every criterion of each against the file. One criterion missed closes that class, however clean the rest; zero debits meeting none is Standard. Points that have decided cases: blood pressure is two criteria (UW-T07: systolic 124 met every class, diastolic 86 closed Preferred Plus and Preferred, Standard Plus was the answer); lipids are two criteria, read on the printed cholesterol and ratio; an A1c of 6.0 or a cholesterol of 215 meets Preferred but not Preferred Plus; tobacco quit over five years is eligible for Preferred and Standard Plus, never Preferred Plus. Written but untested: a single violation leaves a zero-debit file at Standard; any diabetes or apnea diagnosis and any credited liver rise close all three; a family event dropped for age counts against the family criteria (entry 12); build is read on the adjusted weight; a cholesterol above 240 whose band the ratio overrode still closes all three. State the class reached and which criteria closed the classes above it. Drivers empty; modifiers empty.

**Boundary:** Only for a total of zero with no modifier. Any debit closes all three preferred classes; do not work or name the criteria on a charged file, one clause that the charge closes them is enough (UW-T22). A zero-debit pilot is not this entry either: the flat extra makes the file entry 6 and entry 21, and the hazardous avocation closes every preferred class, so the answer is Standard with the modifier (untested). 'Standard' is reached two ways: here by criteria, and under entry 21 as the 0-49 band of a charged total; the name is the same and the drivers differ.

**Why:** UW-T05 (Preferred, material factors none; naming the closing criteria as drivers was the one error) and UW-T07 (Standard Plus, empty list, confirmed). The class on a clean file is decided by the criteria; the drivers record only charges, credits, modifiers and decision-forcing facts, and on a clean file there are none.

## 19. Nothing on the file is referred: say so in one line and rate

**When it applies:** Nothing on the file touches a referral line. The lines and where each is settled: recent change in build (entry 9, a loss); treated blood pressure (entry 11, any treated file); lipid bands that disagree (entry 15); late-onset metabolic disease (entry 10, needs a diagnosis); family history past the qualifying age (entry 12, needs an event); private aviation (entry 6); hazardous occupation with hazardous avocation (entry 5, needs both); recent motoring conviction (entry 2, the DUI line); borderline liver chemistry (entry 8, below twice normal); treated sleep-disordered breathing (entry 7, CPAP in use); face amount large relative to income (entry 1); combination of cardiac risk factors (entries 13 and 14, needs at least one charged cardiac table).

**What to do:** Write one sentence that no line is referred, then rate on the tables and, if every table is zero, on the preferred criteria (entry 18). **Boundary:** Where referral lines are live, dispose of each under its entry with one sentence, apply this entry to every other line, and do not go looking for more. **Why:** UW-T05, UW-T07, UW-T11, UW-T15, UW-T16 and UW-T19 had nothing referred, said so in one sentence, were rated on the tables and were confirmed. Judgement is spent where the manual asks for it and nowhere else.

## 20. Tobacco quit within five years: charge as written, name 'tobacco', the negative screen changes nothing

## 21. A file with table debits: accept at the class the total reads; a flat extra or rider makes it accept with modification

Drivers under entry 16.
