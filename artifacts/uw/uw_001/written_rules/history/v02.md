# House rule book

_Rewritten after UW-T02. Every entry is reviewer-settled practice from an earlier application in this run (UW-T01, UW-T02). The starter manual still governs anything this book does not cover._

## How to use this book

Before rating, list every line of the file that the manual refers to underwriting judgement, and check each one against the entries below. Where an entry applies, follow it instead of the manual's table and instead of your own reasoning. Where no entry applies, rate on the tables and say in the rationale that judgement was used.

Two lessons so far about what a referral line means:

- **It is not permission to charge the full table debit.** On UW-T01, charging every referred item at face value made the file two steps too severe. Where the file documents a mitigating fact (treatment compliance, no alcohol history), the reviewer credits it; where a hazard can be carved out of cover, the reviewer uses a rider rather than a flat extra.
- **It is not permission to wave the item through either.** On UW-T02 the total cover exceeded the income multiple, the manual referred it, and the file was accepted at Standard with a paragraph explaining why the excess was tolerable. The reviewer postponed. Where the referral turns on evidence that is not on the file, the answer is to postpone and ask for it, not to reason your way to an accept.

So a referred item resolves in one of three ways: **credit** it when the file already documents the mitigating fact; **convert** it to an exclusion rider when the hazard can be removed from cover; **postpone** when the item can only be settled by evidence the file does not contain. Charging at face value and accepting on argument are both wrong.

## 1. Total cover above the income multiple: postpone

**When it applies:** The face amount applied for plus cover already in force exceeds the manual's income multiple for the applicant's age (section 14: 18-30 30x | 31-40 25x | 41-50 20x | 51-60 15x | 61+ 10x). Run this check on every file before rating anything else; the in-force amount counts in full.

**What to do:** Postpone. Decision `postpone`, rating class null, no modifiers. Do not rate the file, and do not accept at any class with the excess explained away in the rationale. Say in the rationale that the file is postponed for financial evidence (justification of the amount, verification of income, purpose of cover) and state the total cover, the multiple and the excess. Name face amount to income among the drivers, together with every other factor the tables would have charged (see entry 2).

**Why:** The manual refers a large face amount relative to income to judgement. The reviewer's settled practice is that a large face amount at a high multiple of income is postponed for financial evidence rather than rated: the open question is whether the amount is justified, and nothing on the file can answer it. On UW-T02 (age 40, $1.75M applied plus $250K in force against $68,000 income, a 25x multiple of $1.7M) the excess was roughly a fifth over the multiple, the applicant was otherwise a clean Standard, and the reviewer still postponed. No tolerance margin above the multiple is known to be accepted; do not invent one.

## 2. Drivers on a postponed file

**When it applies:** Any file you postpone.

**What to do:** List as drivers both the reason for the postponement and every factor that would have moved the rating had the file been rated. On UW-T02 that meant face amount to income and tobacco quit within the last five years. Rating class stays null and modifiers stay empty.

**Why:** The reviewer's markup on a postponed file names its material factors as the rating factors plus the postponement reason. A postpone is a deferral, not a clean file; the drivers tell whoever picks the file up again what the rating will turn on once the evidence arrives.

## 3. Hazardous occupation together with a hazardous avocation

**When it applies:** The applicant works in a class C (hazardous) occupation and also carries a hazardous avocation, meaning anything section 10 of the manual would charge: amateur motorsport competition, private aviation, scuba beyond 60 ft, technical rock or ice climbing.

**What to do:** Charge the occupation class debit as the manual sets it. For the avocation, attach an exclusion rider naming the avocation hazard. Do not charge the avocation's flat extra and do not add avocation debits. The rider makes the decision *accept with modification*. Name both the occupation class and the avocation among the drivers: the avocation is still a material factor even though it carries no charge.

**Why:** The manual refers this combination to judgement. The reviewer's settled practice is that once one hazardous exposure is already priced through the occupation class, the second is removed from cover rather than charged on top. On UW-T01 (high-voltage lineman plus club saloon racing, 14 events a year) the $5.00 flat extra was struck and replaced by a motorsport exclusion rider.

## 4. Sleep apnea with documented CPAP compliance

**When it applies:** Sleep apnea is diagnosed and the file documents CPAP compliance.

**What to do:** No debit for sleep apnea. Do not list it as a driver.

**Why:** The manual's charge is for diagnosed apnea as such, and it refers treated sleep-disordered breathing to judgement. The reviewer treats documented compliance as a controlled condition carrying no charge. If compliance is not documented, or the apnea is untreated, charge per the manual.

## 5. Mildly elevated liver enzymes with no alcohol history

**When it applies:** Liver enzymes are elevated but below twice the upper limit of normal, and the file shows no alcohol history and no treatment for alcohol use.

**What to do:** No debit for liver chemistry. Do not list it as a driver.

**Why:** The manual refers borderline liver chemistry to judgement. The reviewer treats a mild elevation with no alcohol history as incidental. Enzymes at or above twice normal, or any alcohol history or treatment, go back to the manual's tables.
