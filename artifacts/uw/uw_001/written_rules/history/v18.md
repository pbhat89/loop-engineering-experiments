# House rule book

_Rewritten after UW-T18. Every entry is reviewer-settled practice from an application already decided in this run (UW-T01 through UW-T18). The starter manual governs anything this book is silent on. Where the book and the manual differ, the book is the reviewer's practice and the book wins: that includes the manual's no-referral fallback for private aviation (entry 6), the manual's line that the applicant's own age never changes the family-history charge (entry 12), how a treated blood pressure is charged and named (entry 11), and the manual's line that both lipid tables are in force (entry 15: where the cholesterol band and the ratio band disagree, the ratio governs and the cholesterol band is not charged)._

## How to use this book

Work every file in this order.

1. **Postponement checks** (entries 1 and 2): total cover against the income multiple; any DUI conviction and its year. If either trips, the decision is `postpone`, rating class null, no modifiers. Do not read a class off the total and do not accept with the problem argued away in the rationale. Still finish steps 2 to 4, because the drivers on a postponed file (entry 3) are every factor that would have charged, taken a credit against the total, or carried a modifier.

2. **Referred items.** List every line the manual refers to underwriting judgement and resolve each under entries 5 to 15. First make sure the line really is referred; the manual's referral words are narrow, and a line outside them is simply charged on its table: apnea with no CPAP in use (entry 7), enzymes at or above twice the upper limit of normal (entry 8), two or more moving violations (entry 4), an A1c with no diagnosis (entry 10), a qualifying family event on an applicant under 60 (entry 12), tobacco quit within five years (entry 20). One referral is easy to under-read because it is printed in the lipids section as a general remark: two lipid tables pointing at different bands is a live referral, and it is settled (entry 15: the ratio governs; the cholesterol band is not charged). Where an entry applies, follow it instead of the manual's table, instead of the manual's own fallback, and instead of your own reasoning. Noticing a referral, writing that judgement was used, and then charging the raw table anyway is the same error as not noticing it: UW-T10 and UW-T18 each lost a band that way. Where the file sits near a settled point but not on it, lean the way the settled cases lean, not back to the raw table: UW-T17 cost a band by drawing the late-onset diabetes line tighter than the reviewer draws it. Where no entry applies, rate on the tables and say judgement was used. Where nothing is referred, say so in one line (entry 19). Each referred line resolves on its own: a credit on one line does not soften another, a charge on one line does not withdraw a credit on another, and one file can carry an adjustment, a credit, charged tables and a modifier at once (UW-T08, UW-T10, UW-T12, UW-T13, UW-T14, UW-T17, UW-T18).

3. **Tables and class.** Charge each table exactly as written, on the reading as entries 7 to 12 and 15 leave it. The driving record is two tables (entry 4). Blood pressure is one charge, never two: the reading table when untreated, the treatment charge when treated (entry 11). Lipids are two charges when the two bands agree and one charge, the ratio's, when they disagree (entry 15). Aviation carries no debits; it is a flat extra (entry 6). Add the debits, take any credit (entry 10), and read the class off the manual's section 2 bands, lower edges included: 25 is Standard (UW-T03, UW-T12, UW-T16), 50 is Table 2 (UW-T15), 100 is Table 4 (UW-T18) and 150 is Table 6 (UW-T14). A total below the decline band is accepted at the class it reads. With a rider or flat extra attached the decision is `accept_with_modification` and the class is still read off the total (entry 21). On a zero-debit file, work the preferred classes under entry 18; any debit at all, one charged table included, takes the file to entry 21 instead.

4. **Drivers** under entry 16. The reviewer checks the material factors as closely as the class; a correct class with the wrong drivers is still a mismatch, and on a postponed file the drivers are the one thing besides the decision the reviewer can check. Three names are easy to get wrong and have been: 'blood pressure treatment' for a treated pressure (not 'blood pressure reading'); 'late-onset diabetes credit' beside 'A1c' whenever entry 10 credits (UW-T17 missed both); and 'lipid ratio' alone, with no 'total cholesterol', when the lipid bands disagree (UW-T18 named both).

## What a referral line means, on eighteen files

- **Not permission to charge the full table debit** (UW-T01: every referred item charged at face value came out two steps too severe). Where the file documents a mitigating fact, the reviewer credits it; where a hazard can be carved out of cover, the reviewer uses a rider rather than a flat extra.
- **Not permission to wave the item through** (UW-T02: cover above the multiple accepted at Standard with a paragraph on why the excess was tolerable; the reviewer postponed). Where the referral turns on evidence not on the file, postpone and ask for it.
- **Not an instruction to add a charge the tables do not carry** (UW-T03, UW-T13, UW-T14: a mild cardiometabolic cluster fell under the combinations referral; build alone was charged, nothing added, all three confirmed).
- **Not a reason to soften a heavy file** (UW-T04: five charged cardiac tables plus a class C occupation summed to Table 8; nothing credited, class confirmed, the combination acknowledged by naming 'cardiac risk interaction').
- **Not discharged by charging the table rate and writing that judgement was used** (UW-T06: a DUI two years old charged at 50 with a reassuring note; the reviewer postponed). Where a referral is about recency, the missing evidence is time.
- **Not something to hunt for on a clean file** (UW-T05, UW-T07, UW-T11, UW-T15, UW-T16: nothing referred, the rationale said so in one sentence, each confirmed). A single charged table on an otherwise spotless file is not a judgement item either: charge it, read the class, name it (UW-T15 moving violations at Table 2; UW-T16 tobacco at Standard).
- **Not a reason to touch the lines that are not referred, in either direction** (UW-T08, UW-T12, UW-T13, UW-T14, UW-T17, UW-T18: a credited or adjusted line beside charged lines each time; the credit did not soften the charges, the charges did not withdraw the credit; on UW-T18 the lipid line resolved to the ratio alone while tobacco and untreated apnea charged in full).
- **Not a reason to stop reading the file once it is postponed** (UW-T09: postponed on a recent DUI; the rationale said what would have charged, the drivers named eight factors and the reviewer matched all eight).
- **Not discharged by rating a referred line on its own raw table and writing that judgement was used** (UW-T10: weight loss, late-onset diabetes and private aviation each rated on the manual's table; one step too severe; all three corrected under entries 9, 10 and 6. UW-T18: cholesterol 323 and ratio 5.3 in different bands, both charged as they stand under a sentence that judgement was used; one step too severe; the ratio governs and the cholesterol band is not charged, entry 15). A referral about one line's own facts is answered by changing how that line is read; the rule that a combination adds nothing is a rule about combinations only.
- **Not discharged by finding the file just outside the book's own boundary and withholding the credit** (UW-T17: diabetes diagnosed at about 49, diet controlled to an A1c of 6.8, called not late-onset because the book had drawn the line at the early fifties; the reviewer credited it and the class came out one step too severe). A settled point is where a credit has been seen, not the edge of where it is given. When a referred line's facts are of the same kind as a settled credit and not plainly different, take the credit and say judgement was used on the boundary.
- **An adjustment is made whichever way it moves the file** (UW-T14: the half add-back for a 28 lb loss raised the build band one step and was confirmed; UW-T17: a 17 lb loss on a printed BMI of 26.9 took build from the zero band into the first charged band and 'build' was a material factor). It is a reading rule, not a concession.
- **A credit reaches exactly as far as the facts that earned it** (UW-T11, UW-T12: enzymes above twice normal with no alcohol history are not borderline; charged and confirmed both times).
- **A charged line is charged once and called by what was charged** (UW-T17: a treated blood pressure carries the treatment charge; the reading is not charged on top and the factor is 'blood pressure treatment'. UW-T18: lipids whose bands disagree carry the ratio charge; the cholesterol is not charged on top and the factor is 'lipid ratio').
- **A material factor is a factor that carried a charge, took a credit against the total, carried a modifier, or forced the decision, and nothing else.** Zero-band readings, credited lines, overridden bands, reassuring facts, hobbies and the applicant's age, sex and product are never named (entry 16).

A referred item therefore resolves in one of five ways. **Credit** it when the file documents the mitigating fact (entries 7, 8, 10, 12). **Adjust how the line is read** before the band is charged: half the year's loss added back (entry 9); the ratio table in place of both lipid tables (entry 15). **Convert it to a modifier** (entries 5 and 6). **Postpone** when the question cannot be answered from the file in front of you, whether evidence is missing (entry 1) or the event is too recent (entry 2). **Rate on the tables as they stand** only when every reading involved is already priced by its own table and the referral is about how they combine (entries 13 and 14), in which case the referral may change the drivers list and nothing else, or when the manual already carries the one charge that fits the state (a treated, controlled blood pressure carries the treatment charge and nothing more, entry 11). A line that only resembles a referred item is not one and is charged like any other. The pattern across eighteen files: where the referral is about the applicant's own documented facts (a loss, a diagnosis date, a controlled reading, CPAP compliance, a mild enzyme rise, an old family event, modest flying, two lipid bands that disagree) the reviewer has adjusted, credited or converted every time and never charged the raw table on top; where it is about evidence not on the file (justification of the amount, the age of a conviction) the reviewer has postponed every time. No rated file has yet been corrected as too lenient.

## 1. Total cover above the income multiple: postpone

**When it applies:** Face amount applied for plus cover already in force exceeds the manual's section 14 multiple for the applicant's age (18-30 30x | 31-40 25x | 41-50 20x | 51-60 15x | 61+ 10x). Run this on every file before rating anything else; the in-force amount counts in full.

**What to do:** Decision `postpone`, rating class null, no modifiers. Do not rate the file and do not accept at any class with the excess explained away. Say in the rationale that the file is postponed for financial evidence (justification of the amount, verification of income, purpose of cover), and state the total cover, the multiple and the excess. Name face amount to income among the drivers, together with every factor that would have rated (entry 3).

**Why:** The manual refers a large face amount to judgement; the reviewer's practice is that cover above the multiple is postponed for financial evidence, because the open question (is the amount justified) is not answered by anything on the file. On UW-T02 (age 40, $1.75M applied plus $250K in force against $68,000 income, a 25x ceiling of $1.7M) the excess was about a fifth over, the applicant was otherwise a clean Standard, and the reviewer still postponed. No tolerance above the multiple is known; do not invent one.

## 2. Recent DUI conviction: postpone

**When it applies:** The file shows a DUI conviction and the conviction is recent. On both files decided so far a conviction two years before the application (convicted 2024, file dated 2026) was recent. Run this alongside the financial check. It trips on its own: a clean MVR, normal liver chemistry and an empty alcohol history do not switch it off (UW-T06), and a heavy file with several charged tables does not turn it back into a rating (UW-T09).

**What to do:** Decision `postpone`, rating class null, no modifiers. Do not charge the DUI at the manual's 50 and read a class, and do not accept with a note that judgement was used. In the rationale, set the conviction year against the application date; say the file is postponed for the conviction to age and for the record around it (court disposition, licence status, any alcohol assessment or treatment); say plainly that normal liver chemistry and no declared alcohol history do not settle the question. Then finish the file: place cover inside the multiple in one line (entry 17) and say which other lines would have charged, what would have been credited and how each referred item would have been handled. Name 'DUI' among the drivers with every factor that would have rated (entry 3).

**Boundary:** The manual's 50 is the rate for a conviction that is not recent; the section 11 referral for a recent motoring conviction sends a recent one to judgement, and judgement postpones it. Two years is the only point settled, twice over. Until a case settles an older conviction, treat a DUI within the last two years as recent; for an older one, charge 50, name 'DUI', and say judgement was used and the conviction was treated as not recent. Ordinary moving violations are a separate table (entry 4): never a reason to postpone, and they make a DUI neither more nor less recent (UW-T06 had a clean MVR, UW-T09 two violations; same decision).

**Why:** UW-T06 (male 37; DUI convicted 2024, no violations; normal enzymes, no alcohol history; apnea with no CPAP in use; all else zero) was accepted at Table 4 on DUI plus apnea and the reviewer postponed it: a recent DUI is postponed, not rated. UW-T09 (male 44; DUI convicted 2024 with two violations; 147/83 untreated; 268 with ratio 5.3; A1c 6.8 no diagnosis; private pilot, class A) was postponed on the DUI with eight drivers and the reviewer confirmed both. Recency is settled by time, and nothing on today's file stands in for it.

## 3. Drivers on a postponed file

**When it applies:** Any file postponed under entry 1 or 2.

**What to do:** List the reason for the postponement and every factor that would have been material had the file been rated, built exactly as entry 16 builds them: one per table that would have charged, in the reviewer's names; 'aviation' where entry 6 would have attached its flat extra, or the avocation where entry 5 would have attached a rider; 'late-onset diabetes credit' where entry 10 would apply; 'cardiac risk interaction' where entry 14 would name it; the lipid line as entry 15 would leave it. UW-T02: face amount to income and tobacco. UW-T06: DUI and sleep apnea. UW-T09: DUI, blood pressure reading, total cholesterol, lipid ratio, A1c, moving violations, aviation and cardiac risk interaction; the reviewer's list matched exactly. Rating class stays null, modifiers empty. Do not list zero-band readings, credited lines or reassuring facts.

**Why:** The reviewer's markup on a postponed file names the postponement reason plus the factors that would have rated, and treats a referred avocation and the cardiac interaction exactly as on a rated file. A postpone defers the class, not the underwriting.

## 4. Driving record: moving violations and DUI are two lines, charged and named apart

**When it applies:** Every file; section 11 of the manual.

**What to do:** Read the three-year moving-violations count and the DUI line as two separate tables. Zero or one violation is the zero band: charge nothing, name nothing. Two violations is the first charged band and three or more the second: charge exactly as written and name 'moving violations' (the reviewer's phrase). A DUI is its own line: recent, postpone under entry 2; not recent, charge 50 and name 'DUI'. Where a charged violations count and a DUI sit together, name both as two drivers.

**Boundary:** Moving violations are never a referred item and never a reason to postpone, whatever the count: three in three years is the second charged band, not the manual's 'recent motoring conviction', which is about the DUI line (UW-T15, UW-T17). Do not fold the violations into the DUI or the DUI into the violations. Two or more violations carry a debit, so such a file never reaches the preferred criteria (entry 18) and goes to entry 21, however clean everything else is. A single violation on an otherwise zero-debit file leaves it at Standard by criterion (entry 18); a single violation on a file with a charge elsewhere is nothing (UW-T12). A clean or nearly clean record is not named and is not a credit against anything. The violations line is not a cardiac table (entry 14).

**Why:** UW-T08 and UW-T12 each carried one violation: zero band, not named, both confirmed. UW-T09 carried two violations and a 2024 DUI: 'moving violations' and 'DUI' named as two separate factors on the postponed file. UW-T10 carried two violations, no DUI, on a rated file: charged as written, named, among the reviewer's six factors. UW-T15 (male 31, 10-year term, cover inside the multiple; three violations in three years, no DUI; every other table zero, never-smoker with a negative screen, no referred line) was charged at the second band, the only debit on the file; the total of 50 read Table 2, the file was accepted with no modifier and 'moving violations' as the only driver, and the reviewer confirmed it. UW-T17 carried three violations on a heavy file: second band, 'moving violations' among the reviewer's six factors. UW-T11, UW-T13, UW-T14, UW-T16 and UW-T18 carried none and nothing was named.

## 5. Hazardous occupation together with a hazardous avocation: rider, not flat extra

**When it applies:** A class C occupation together with an avocation section 10 would charge: amateur motorsport competition, private aviation, scuba beyond 60 ft, technical rock or ice climbing.

**What to do:** Charge the occupation class debit as the manual sets it. For the avocation, attach an exclusion rider naming the avocation hazard; charge no flat extra and no avocation debits. The rider makes the decision `accept_with_modification`. Name both 'occupation class' and the avocation among the drivers: the avocation is material though it carries no charge.

**Boundary:** A class C occupation on its own is ordinary rating: charge the class debit, no rider, name 'occupation class' (UW-T04 logging feller; UW-T13 structural steel erector, where the charge stood beside a credited family-history line). A hazardous avocation with a class A or B occupation is not this combination: private aviation alone goes to entry 6; the other section 10 avocations are charged as their table reads. A pastime section 10 does not list is not an avocation and is not named anywhere, however long it has been kept up: stained-glass work (UW-T04), growing chillies (UW-T05), collecting crime novels (UW-T06, UW-T10), a book club (UW-T07; nine years running on UW-T16 and UW-T18), a rugby season ticket (UW-T08), a community choir (UW-T09), a pub quiz league (UW-T11), classical guitar (UW-T13, UW-T17), beekeeping (UW-T14), weekend sourdough baking (UW-T15); an incidental detail that is not even a pastime (a pet on UW-T12) is nothing at all. Do not reason a hobby into a hazard and do not read steadiness of character into a rating.

**Why:** The manual refers the combination to judgement. The reviewer's practice: once one hazardous exposure is priced through the occupation class, the second is removed from cover rather than charged on top. On UW-T01 (high-voltage lineman plus club saloon racing, 14 events a year) the $5.00 flat extra was struck and replaced by a motorsport exclusion rider.

## 6. Private aviation without a hazardous occupation: first-band flat extra, no debits, never a decline

**When it applies:** The applicant flies as a private pilot and the occupation is class A or B. The manual's fallback (rate as a hazardous avocation, 50) is not the reviewer's practice; this entry replaces it.

**What to do:** Charge no debits for aviation on any table. Attach a flat extra at the first band of section 15 (amount_per_1000 2.50) with the detail naming private aviation. The decision is `accept_with_modification`; the class is read off the total of the other tables with no aviation debit in it. Name 'aviation' among the drivers. Say in one sentence that aviation was considered under judgement, the hours are modest, and it carries a first-band flat extra and no rating debits. Never decline for the flying alone.

**Boundary:** Modest hours: 82 a year (351 total, no aerobatics) on UW-T10 took the first band; UW-T09 flew 75 a year and aviation was named on that postponed file. A much heavier programme, aerobatics or a very thin logbook has no settled practice: still no debits, still a flat extra, never a decline; say judgement was used on the band and do not step up without a case. Do not scale within the modest range and do not credit a long uneventful logbook. With a class C occupation, entry 5 converts the avocation to a rider instead. Aviation is not a cardiac table (entry 14). A pilot's file never reaches a preferred class.

**Why:** On UW-T10 (male 56, schoolteacher, private pilot 82 hours a year) aviation was charged the manual's fallback 50 and the total read Table 8; the reviewer's markup: one step too severe, accept with modification at Table 6 with a first-band flat extra; private aviation at modest hours takes a flat extra rather than debits and is never declined for the flying alone. UW-T09 had already named 'aviation' as a material factor on a postponed file.

## 7. Sleep apnea: credited to zero with documented CPAP compliance, charged as written without it

**When it applies:** Sleep apnea is diagnosed.

**What to do:** With documented CPAP compliance: no debit, not a driver; one sentence that judgement was used and treated apnea was credited to zero. With no CPAP in use or compliance not documented: charge the manual's 50, name 'sleep apnea', say in one line it is charged as written. Each side holds whatever else the file does: the charge stood beside a postponement (UW-T06), a credited liver line (UW-T08), a build charged on an adjusted weight (UW-T14), a credited A1c with a treated pressure (UW-T17) and a lipid line resolved to the ratio alone (UW-T18); the credit stood beside a charged liver line (UW-T12).

**Boundary:** 'No CPAP in use' is untreated apnea however clean the rest of the file and is not the 'treated sleep-disordered breathing' referral: nothing to refer, only the table to charge. Do not postpone for a sleep study or treatment evidence, and add nothing for apnea beside an obese build. A diagnosis, treated or not, closes all three preferred classes by criterion, so a file whose only abnormality is compliant apnea is a zero-debit Standard (entry 18; untested). Where no apnea is reported the line is zero and not named. Apnea is not a cardiac table (entries 13 and 14).

**Why:** The reviewer treats documented compliance as a controlled condition carrying no charge: set on UW-T01, confirmed on UW-T12 (compliant apnea credited beside charged enzymes; Standard; liver enzymes the only factor). Untreated apnea is charged exactly as written: UW-T06 (named on the postponed file), UW-T08 (Table 2 with tobacco), UW-T14 (Table 6 with build), UW-T17 (Table 6 among six factors), UW-T18 (Table 4 with lipid ratio and tobacco).

## 8. Liver chemistry: a mild rise with no alcohol history is credited to zero; at or above twice normal it is charged and named 'liver enzymes'

**When it applies:** Section 12: elevated liver enzymes, a history of treatment for alcohol use, or both.

**What to do:** Read how far the enzymes are elevated and whether there is any alcohol history or treatment.

- **Elevated below twice the upper limit of normal, no alcohol history, no treatment:** the borderline chemistry the manual refers; credit to zero, no debit, not a driver; one sentence that judgement was used and the elevation was treated as incidental.
- **Elevated at or above twice the upper limit of normal, no alcohol history, no treatment:** not borderline, not referred, no credit. Charge the manual's elevated-enzymes debit as written and name 'liver enzymes'. The empty alcohol history is a fact the mild-elevation credit needs, not a credit of its own; it does not soften the charge and is not named. Add nothing for the size of the rise, do not postpone for a repeat panel, do not decline. Where this is the only charge the total reads Standard and 'liver enzymes' is the only driver (UW-T12).
- **Normal chemistry, no alcohol history:** the zero state (UW-T13 through UW-T18 and most files). Charge nothing, name nothing, and do not spend it elsewhere: it does not offset a DUI (entry 2).
- **Any history of treatment for alcohol use** (untested): charge the treatment debit as written and name it; where enzymes are also elevated both section 12 lines charge and a mild rise is not credited, because the credit exists for a rise with no alcohol behind it. Say judgement was used on the naming.

**Boundary:** The credit and the charge are both local to the liver line (UW-T08, UW-T12). Each preferred class needs normal liver chemistry and no alcohol history, so a mild rise credited to zero still closes all three preferred classes on an otherwise zero-debit file (entry 18; untested). Liver enzymes are not a cardiac table (UW-T11).

**Why:** Mild elevation with no alcohol history treated as incidental: UW-T01 and UW-T08 (male 56, enzymes below twice normal; Table 2 confirmed on tobacco and apnea alone). The other side settled on UW-T11 (male 32; enzymes above twice normal, no alcohol history; tobacco quit within five years; Table 2 confirmed with tobacco and liver enzymes) and UW-T12 (female 32; the same enzyme picture standing alone; Standard confirmed with liver enzymes the only factor).

## 9. Weight lost in the last twelve months: add half back before reading the build band, whichever way it moves the file

**When it applies:** The twelve-month weight change is a loss, whatever its size and whatever the printed BMI, a printed BMI in the zero band included. Section 3 refers a recent change in build; this is the settled answer for a loss (UW-T10, UW-T14, UW-T17).

**What to do:** Add half the year's loss back onto the current weight, recompute the BMI at the file's height, and read the section 3 band off the recomputed BMI, not the printed one. Where the band charges, name 'build'; the adjustment is not a separate driver and 'weight change' or 'weight loss' is never named. One sentence: weight down so many pounds in the year, half added back under judgement, adjusted weight and BMI so much, band read accordingly. Do it whichever way it moves the file; do not credit the loss as an improvement, do not skip it because it raises the class or because the printed BMI is normal, do not postpone for the weight to prove stable.

**Boundary:** Half is the fraction for a 16 lb loss (UW-T10: 65 inches, 200 lb, adjusted 208, BMI about 34.6), a 28 lb loss (UW-T14: 67 inches, 207 lb, adjusted 221, BMI about 34.6) and a 17 lb loss (UW-T17: 63 inches, 152 lb, printed BMI 26.9, adjusted 160.5, BMI about 28.4) alike. On the first two the band moved from 31.0-33.9 to 34.0-36.9; on UW-T17 it moved from the zero band into 28.0-30.9, so the add-back put a charge on a file whose printed build carried none, and the reviewer listed 'build' first among the material factors. Do not scale the fraction. Where the adjusted BMI stays in the printed band, still do the add-back and say so. A stable weight is not a recent change in build and is read as it stands (UW-T03, UW-T05, UW-T07, UW-T08, UW-T11, UW-T12, UW-T13, UW-T15, UW-T16, UW-T18). Weight gained in the year has no settled practice: read the current BMI, subtract nothing, say judgement was used. An adjusted BMI reaching the decline band is untested; the manual's table would apply. Build on the adjusted weight is one cardiac table for entries 13 and 14. On a zero-debit file the adjusted BMI, not the printed one, is the figure the preferred criteria are read against (entry 18; untested).

**Why:** UW-T10's markup: weight lost in the past year is added back at half before the build band is read. UW-T14 showed the add-back standing alone, raising the class from Table 4 to Table 6, and the reviewer confirmed the heavier class with build as a material factor. UW-T17's markup repeated the rule word for word on a file whose printed BMI was 26.9. Recent loss is not yet durable; half is the settled fraction.

## 10. Type 2 diabetes first diagnosed in later middle age with a well-controlled A1c: charge the A1c, take a credit, name both

**When it applies:** A type 2 diabetes diagnosis; the year of diagnosis puts the applicant in later middle age at the time; the A1c is well controlled. Settled points: UW-T10, diagnosed 2024 at about 54, diet controlled, A1c 6.6; UW-T17, diagnosed 2015 at about 49, diet controlled, A1c 6.8. Work the age at diagnosis from the diagnosis year and the applicant's present age. The applicant's present age (56, 60) and the years since diagnosis (two on UW-T10, eleven on UW-T17) do not matter.

**What to do:** Charge the A1c on its section 6 table as written. Take a credit against the total for the late-onset, well-controlled disease and read the class off the credited total. Name 'A1c' and 'late-onset diabetes credit' as two separate drivers. One sentence: diagnosed in such a year at such an age, controlled by such means to such an A1c; A1c charged; late-onset credit taken under judgement.

**Size of the credit:** The markup gives no point value. On both settled files the uncredited debits summed to exactly 200, the foot of Table 8, and the reviewer's class was Table 6, one band below. Work the credit as one band below what the uncredited total reads, and say the size is judgement.

**Boundary:** An A1c with no diagnosis is never this entry, whatever the reading: in the zero band (UW-T07, UW-T12 at 6.1, UW-T13 at 5.8, UW-T14 at 6.0, UW-T15 at 5.4, UW-T16 at 5.5, UW-T18 at 6.0) it is nothing, not named even where it misses a preferred threshold; in a charged band with no diagnosis (UW-T04 at 6.6, UW-T09 at 6.8) it is charged on its table with no credit. Age at diagnosis: 49 and 54 are settled, so a diagnosis at any age from the late forties on is later middle age. The book once drew this line at the early fifties and lost a band for it (UW-T17); do not draw it tight again. A diagnosis in the twenties or thirties is not later middle age: charge, no credit, say judgement was used. The early-to-mid forties have no case; from about 45 take the credit and say judgement was used on the age, below that do not. Control: 6.6 and 6.8 are settled, so the whole 6.5-6.9 band under diet control is well controlled. An A1c of 7.0 or above with a diagnosis has no settled practice: charge the band, take no credit, say judgement was used. Oral medication with an A1c in 6.5-6.9 is untested; the condition is control, not means, so take the credit and say judgement was used. A diagnosed diabetic with an A1c at or under 6.4 has nothing to charge or credit; the diagnosis closes all three preferred classes, so such a zero-debit file is Standard. The credit is against the total, not a cancellation of the A1c line: the line stays charged and stays a cardiac table for entry 14.

**Why:** On UW-T10 the A1c was charged with a note that judgement was used and the reviewer corrected it: adult-onset diabetes first diagnosed in later middle age with a well-controlled A1c earns a credit against the debits; the material factors included both 'A1c' and 'late-onset diabetes credit'. On UW-T17 (female 60; diagnosed 2015, diet controlled, A1c 6.8) the credit was withheld because 49 was called not late-onset, the total of 200 was read as Table 8, and the reviewer corrected it in the same words to Table 6 with 'late-onset diabetes credit' among the six material factors.
